The Korine / Gallo lane 1980 to now No Korine. No Gallo.

Thirty films that live where Gummo and Buffalo '66 live: outsider lyricism, hangout misery, transgression as form, non-actors, damaged film stock, and the feeling that the movie was found rather than made. Their actual filmographies are banned from this list, which is the whole point.

RULE 1 1980 or later RULE 2 No Korine, no Gallo credits as director RULE 3 If your video store filed it under "misc," it qualifies
'80s
Films 01-09 · The bootleg era

Where the lane starts: midnight movies, Euro extremity, and 16mm made by people with no permission and no adults in the room.

01

Forbidden Zone

Richard Elfman · 1980 · USA

A family's basement door leads to the Sixth Dimension, ruled by a lecherous midget king, staged as a black-and-white cartoon-vaudeville fever with Oingo Boingo doing the songs. Handmade, offensive, and unrepeatable.

The DIY musical-grotesque energy Korine borrows whenever he lets his freaks sing.
Midnight MovieHandmade
02

Pixote

Héctor Babenco · 1980 · Brazil

A ten-year-old street kid drifts from a brutal reformatory into São Paulo's crime economy, played by an actual street kid, Fernando Ramos da Silva, whose real life ended the way the film fears. Neorealism with the safety rails removed.

The non-actor, real-kids-in-real-ruin method that Kids and Gummo industrialized fifteen years later.
Non-ActorsFoundational
03

Possession

Andrzej Żuławski · 1981 · France / West Germany

A marriage disintegrates in divided Berlin until the wife's secret turns out to be a tentacled thing in a rented flat. Isabelle Adjani's subway breakdown is the most committed three minutes of acting on film.

Emotional maximalism as transgression: the film every art-damage director secretly measures intensity against.
Euro ExtremityBanned in the UK
04

Liquid Sky

Slava Tsukerman · 1982 · USA

Aliens land on a Manhattan rooftop to harvest the brain chemicals of new-wave club kids at the moment of orgasm. Anne Carlisle plays both leads in a neon-smeared no-budget downtown artifact.

Scene-documentation disguised as sci-fi: the club-kid anthropology Korine's whole crowd grew up bootlegging.
No BudgetScene Artifact
05

Angst

Gerald Kargl · 1983 · Austria

A killer walks out of prison and into a random house, narrating his compulsions while a body-mounted camera orbits him like a parasite. One feature, then Kargl never directed again.

Formal invention in service of the unwatchable; Noé calls it a direct influence, and the whole provocation wing descends from it.
One and DoneCamera as Weapon
06

Combat Shock

Buddy Giovinazzo · 1986 · USA

A Vietnam vet wanders Staten Island's dead zones with a mutant baby at home, no money, and no exits, shot on scraps and sold by Troma with a lying poster. Taxi Driver rebuilt out of garbage.

Poverty-row despair as texture: the rotting-America mise-en-scène Gummo turned into tornado country.
Poverty RowTroma Orphan
07

River's Edge

Tim Hunter · 1986 · USA

A teenager kills his girlfriend, shows the body to his friends, and nobody does anything. Crispin Glover vibrates at a frequency the rest of the cast can't hear; Dennis Hopper romances a sex doll.

The affectless-teen ur-text: Kids, Bully, and every dead-eyed adolescent hangout film start here.
Dead-Eyed TeensUr-Text
08

Nekromantik

Jörg Buttgereit · 1987 · West Germany

A street cleaner brings his work home to complete a very committed love triangle. Super 8 transgression with a mournful score, made to test what cinema is allowed to hold, and seized by police accordingly.

Transgression as manifesto: the underground's line-in-the-sand film, passed hand to hand like contraband.
Super 8Confiscated
09

Tetsuo: The Iron Man

Shinya Tsukamoto · 1989 · Japan

A salaryman hits a metal fetishist with his car and begins mutating into scrap iron, rendered in convulsive 16mm stop-motion and industrial noise. Sixty-seven minutes that feel like being fed through a fax machine.

One-man-band filmmaking at maximum violence: proof the lane's real currency is total authorship, not money.
One-Man BandIndustrial
'90s
Films 10-18 · The art-damage decade

The decade Korine and Gallo actually emerged from. These are their neighbors, rivals, and the films playing in the same downtown theaters.

10

The Reflecting Skin

Philip Ridley · 1990 · UK / Canada

A boy in a golden 1950s wheat-field America decides the widow next door is a vampire while his brother (a young Viggo Mortensen) rots from atomic test exposure. Exploding frogs, dead friends, unbearable beauty.

American-gothic childhood shot like a hallucination: the prestige-painterly end of the outsider-lyric spectrum.
American GothicOutsider Lyric
11

Begotten

E. Elias Merhige · 1990 · USA

God disembowels himself; Mother Earth and her convulsing Son wander a ruined landscape. No dialogue, rephotographed frame by frame until the image is mostly grain and scar tissue.

Film stock as wound. The degraded-image fetish that runs from here straight through every VHS-damage aesthetic since.
No DialogueImage Damage
12

Man Bites Dog

Belvaux, Bonzel & Poelvoorde · 1992 · Belgium

A documentary crew follows a charming serial killer on his rounds and slides from observing to helping. Shot for nothing in black and white by three film students who cast themselves.

Complicity as the subject: the camera-as-accomplice question every provocation film has to answer since.
MockumentaryStudent Film
13

Bad Boy Bubby

Rolf de Heer · 1993 · Australia

A man kept in one room for 35 years by his mother escapes into Adelaide, parroting everything he hears, and accidentally becomes a rock star. Shot with 32 different cinematographers and binaural microphones in the lead's hair.

Holy-fool cinema: the innocent-monster protagonist Korine keeps rewriting, done first and meaner.
Holy Fool32 DPs
14

Clean, Shaven

Lodge Kerrigan · 1993 · USA

A schizophrenic man searches for his daughter while a detective tracks him for a murder he may not have committed. Sound design puts you inside the static of his head; two years to shoot, no compromises.

Subjective damage rendered as pure film form; the craft ceiling of the entire micro-budget misery genre.
Sound as PsychosisMicro-Budget
15

The Doom Generation

Gregg Araki · 1995 · USA

Two teens and a drifter leave a trail of severed heads across a neon-signed nowhere America, every purchase totaling $6.66. Subtitled "A Heterosexual Movie by Gregg Araki" out of pure spite.

Trash aesthetics deployed with total control: nihilism as pop art, the exact register Korine's Trash Humpers pretends not to want.
New Queer CinemaNihilist Pop
16

Trees Lounge

Steve Buscemi · 1996 · USA

An unemployed mechanic drinks his life away at the end-of-the-bar stool in his Long Island hometown, driving an ice cream truck toward one very bad decision. Buscemi writing, directing, and starring as his own worst-case self.

The hangout-misery blueprint Buffalo '66 runs on: autobiography as self-indictment, charm as the trap.
Hangout MiserySelf-Portrait
17

Nil by Mouth

Gary Oldman · 1997 · UK

A South London family orbits an alcoholic patriarch whose violence arrives like weather. Oldman wrote and directed it from his own childhood and never made another film; Ray Winstone has never been scarier.

Autobiographical wound-cinema with zero self-protection, the sincerity standard the ironists in this lane get measured against.
One and DoneKitchen Sink
18

I Stand Alone

Gaspar Noé · 1998 · France

An unemployed horse butcher monologues his hatred of France, family, and himself toward an act the film warns you about with a 30-second on-screen countdown to leave the theater. Noé before the strobe lights.

Direct-address misanthropy as endurance test; the confrontation contract between film and audience, printed in bold.
Endurance TestCountdown Warning
'00s
Films 19-25 · Post-Gummo, pre-streaming

The generation that saw Gummo and Buffalo '66 in theaters and answered back, plus the Euro and Asian wings pushing further out.

19

George Washington

David Gordon Green · 2000 · USA

Kids in a rusting North Carolina rail town cover up an accidental death over one hazy summer, narrated like a dream. Non-actors, anamorphic 35mm, Terrence Malick light poured over Gummo geography.

The direct answer record: Gummo's wasteland reshot as elegy instead of provocation. Same lane, opposite polarity.
Non-ActorsGummo's Mirror
20

Visitor Q

Takashi Miike · 2001 · Japan

A stranger with a rock inserts himself into a family already violating every taboo on the books, and somehow heals them by escalating. Shot on consumer DV in eight days for a V-cinema series.

Domestic transgression played as deadpan sitcom; the furthest anyone has taken the home-video family portrait.
Shot on DVTaboo Bingo
21

Bully

Larry Clark · 2001 · USA

Florida teens talk themselves into murdering the friend who torments them, based on an actual 1993 case, played with terrifying vacancy by Brad Renfro and Bijou Phillips. Clark without Korine's script, which is exactly why it qualifies.

The Kids method aimed at a true crime: affectless adolescence carried to its documented conclusion.
True CrimeClark, No Korine
22

Twentynine Palms

Bruno Dumont · 2003 · France / USA

A photographer and his girlfriend drift through the Joshua Tree desert in a Hummer, eating, fighting, and screwing, until the film detonates in its last ten minutes. Critics compared it to The Brown Bunny all year, mostly as an insult.

Durational drift as a loaded gun; the Gallo-adjacent test of how much nothing an audience will sit through before the something.
Desert DriftSlow Detonation
23

Taxidermia

György Pálfi · 2006 · Hungary

Three generations of Hungarian men: a lecherous orderly, a competitive speed-eater, and a taxidermist who performs the ultimate self-portrait. History as digestion, rendered in grotesque tableaux of real formal beauty.

Body horror as national epic; proof the gross-out can carry generational weight when the craft is total.
Body GrotesqueState Funded, Somehow
24

Ex Drummer

Koen Mortier · 2007 · Belgium

A famous writer joins a band of self-described handicapped Flemish lowlifes as their drummer, purely to study them like insects, and the contempt flows both ways. Opens with a backwards-walking upside-down tracking shot and gets less polite from there.

Class tourism indicted from inside: the author-slumming-with-freaks setup that shadows this whole lane, made literal.
Punk SqualorDeep Cut
25

Frownland

Ronald Bronstein · 2007 · USA

A door-to-door coupon salesman who cannot finish a sentence claws at every social interaction in an airless Brooklyn. Shot on 16mm over years for nothing; Bronstein went on to co-write Good Time and Uncut Gems.

The Safdie cinema's buried root: abrasion as empathy, a protagonist the film refuses to make watchable and dares you to abandon.
16mmSafdie Root System
'10s+
Films 26-30 · The lane goes digital

Post-VHS, post-irony, post-everything. The transgression migrates to anti-comedy, silence, and the internet's id.

26

The Comedy

Rick Alverson · 2012 · USA

An aging Williamsburg trust-fund heir (Tim Heidecker) drifts through Brooklyn testing how cruel his irony can get before anything feels like anything. Sundance walkouts, furious reviews, and a title that is the film's meanest joke.

Buffalo '66's wounded narcissist updated for the irony economy: privilege as the new squalor.
Anti-ComedyWalkout Bait
27

Buzzard

Joel Potrykus · 2014 · USA

A Michigan temp worker running penny-ante scams (returning stolen checks, gaming refund policies) spirals into paranoia with a modified Freddy Krueger power glove. Includes an unbroken, legendary scene of a man eating a plate of spaghetti.

Slacker cinema with actual menace; the microwave-dinner realism of Gummo relocated to dead-mall Michigan.
Dirtbag RealismSpaghetti Scene
28

The Tribe

Myroslav Slaboshpytskyi · 2014 · Ukraine

A new student at a boarding school for the deaf climbs its internal crime syndicate. Entirely in unsubtitled Ukrainian sign language, all deaf non-professional actors, long takes, no music, no mercy.

The formal dare taken to its endpoint: a film that removes every access ramp and trusts pure image to carry violence and desire.
No SubtitlesNon-Actors
29

Kuso

Flying Lotus · 2017 · USA

Post-earthquake Los Angeles, everyone covered in boils, told in interlocking sketches broadcast from a dying television. Sundance's "grossest movie ever made" headlines did the marketing for free.

Trash Humpers' true heir without the Korine credit: channel-surfing as form, the internet's id given a body.
Sketch NightmareMusician's Film
30

Possum

Matthew Holness · 2018 · UK

A disgraced puppeteer returns to his childhood home carrying a bag containing a spider-legged puppet with his own face, which will not stay destroyed. From the creator of Garth Marenghi, played with zero laughs on purpose.

Comedy-brain turned fully against itself: shame and repressed abuse rendered as a physical object you cannot throw away.
Puppet DreadAnti-Comedy

Viewing logic

The connective threads: non-actors and real damage (Pixote, George Washington, The Tribe, Bully), hangout misery (Trees Lounge, Buzzard, The Comedy), transgression as contract (Angst, Nekromantik, I Stand Alone, Visitor Q), image damage as texture (Begotten, Tetsuo, Kuso), and the one-film-and-out auteurs (Angst, Nil by Mouth, Frownland's Bronstein as director).

Entry ramps by tolerance: start gentle with River's Edge, Trees Lounge, George Washington, and Possum. Mid-tier: Bad Boy Bubby, The Reflecting Skin, Buzzard, The Comedy. The deep end is Angst, Nekromantik, Visitor Q, and Kuso, and no one will blame you for tapping out.

Deliberate exclusions beyond the two named bans: Kids and Ken Park are out because Korine wrote them; Bully stays because he didn't. Nothing pre-1980, which spares you the entire Cinema of Transgression short-film rabbit hole (Nick Zedd, Richard Kern), though that is the primordial soup this list crawled out of.
