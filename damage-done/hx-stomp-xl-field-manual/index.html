Working the
Stomp XL A fluency guide and a plan to rebuild the whole unit

The factory content is a demo reel, not a rig. This document has two halves. The first is how to move around the box without thinking about it. The second is how to strip it down to eight presets that actually belong to you, level matched, with the same switch meaning the same thing everywhere.

Orientation

The control map

Two rows of four switches. The outer columns are fixed function, the middle four are yours. Everything above the switches is hand control, and almost all of it is a shortcut you do not know yet.

The one thing to internalize

The six switches are not fixed to a job. What they do depends entirely on which footswitch mode you are in, and the same physical switch means a preset in one mode, an effect in another, and a parameter in a third. Every navigation problem on this pedal is really a question of which mode you are standing in.

Architecture

Three layers of control and what each is actually for

This is the only decision that matters. Get it right and the pedal disappears under your feet. Get it wrong and you spend gigs tap dancing.

Layer 1

Presets

128 slots, 32 banks of four. A whole rig: blocks, chain order, models, all four snapshots, every switch assignment.

Cost: changing preset creates a small audible gap and kills delay and reverb trails. That gap is inherent to how much the box has to reallocate.

Use forBetween songs. Different amp character. Never mid phrase.
Layer 2

Snapshots

Four per preset. Recalls bypass states, up to 64 controller assigned parameter values, Command Center values, and tempo if you set tempo per snapshot.

Cannot swap a model or reorder the chain. All four snapshots share the same blocks.

Use forSong sections. Verse to chorus to solo. Seamless, trails intact, one tap.
Layer 3

Stomps

Individual on and off, like a pedalboard. Up to eight assignments per switch, and a switch can toggle a parameter between two values instead of bypassing a block.

Math: five independent stomps give 32 combinations. Four snapshots give four states.

Use forSpontaneity. The thing you did not plan. Anything you want to feel like a pedal.

The argument, and who is on which side

Joe Gore, who designs stompboxes and did sound design work on the Helix platform, makes the snapshot case on efficiency: a sound that needs four separate stomp taps to assemble is one snapshot tap instead. Alex Strabala, who sells presets professionally, goes the other way and says he uses all his stomps for the combinatorial range and just turns the top knob to change presets. Both are right about their own gigs.

What most working players actually do, and what you should do, is run both in one preset. Snapshots carry the planned section changes because they are gapless and preserve trails. Stomps carry the unplanned. The global setting that arranges which row is which is under Global Settings > Footswitches.

Recommended default for your rig

One preset per amp character. Four snapshots mapped to rhythm base, lifted, dirty, ambient lead. The remaining switches as stomps for a boost, a modulation, and a delay you can grab at will. Preset changes only between songs.

Deep dive

Snapshots

Think of them as four saved states of the same rig, recalled instantly and silently. Not four rigs.

Stored and recalled

  • On and off state of every block except the Looper
  • Values of any parameter assigned to a controller, up to 64 per preset
  • Command Center message values and toggle states
  • Tempo, but only if you set Tempo Select to Per Snapshot

Not stored, ever

  • Which model sits in a block. All four snapshots share the same models.
  • Chain order or routing
  • Any parameter you have not assigned to a controller

Workaround for the first one: load both models as separate blocks and let snapshots turn one on and the other off. Costs you a block and some DSP.

Three global settings to change before you build anything

Snapshot Edits
Global Settings > Preferences. Default is Recall, which keeps accidental knob turns forever. Set it to Discard and your saved snapshots become bulletproof: anything you fiddle with reverts when you leave. The trap: with Discard on, you must save before changing snapshots or your real edits vanish too. The camera icon on the home screen shows which mode you are in, gray for Recall and red for Discard. Shortcut to flip it: hold BYPASS and press SAVE.
Snapshot Reselect
Set to Toggle instead of Reload. Now pressing the already active snapshot flips back to the previous one, so verse and chorus become one switch you rock between instead of two switches you hunt for.
Snapshot Mode return
Global Settings > Footswitches. Set to Manual Return or Latch if you want to stay in Snapshot mode after picking one, rather than the switches vanishing on you.

The four snapshot ceiling

Helix Floor gets eight. You get four. In practice four covers a song's sections when you pair them with stomps, which is why the hybrid layout matters more on the XL than on the big units. If you find yourself consistently needing five or more distinct seamless states inside one song, that is the honest signal to either go one preset per song or accept a gap between sections.

Hazard

Modes, and the MODE switch problem

Four footswitch modes live in Play view: Stomp, Preset, Snapshot and Pedal Edit. Plus Looper mode when a Looper block is engaged. MODE is the only door between them, which makes it the single highest traffic control on the pedal and also its biggest liability.

Known failure, documented

There is a Line 6 forum thread from a player with fifty years of gigging who hit MODE mid set and lost his lead sound. The official answer from a Line 6 Expert was blunt: you cannot disable it in firmware. That is still true on 3.80.

The remedies that actually work, in order of how much they help:

  • Physical block. The original poster solved it with a snap on plastic collar around the switch. The cheaper version is the Grolsch washer trick, a rubber bottle cap washer that raises the switch height enough that a stray foot misses it.
  • Make the press harmless. Put your snapshots into Stomp mode via Command Center, or set the row arrangement so your working switches stay visible in either mode. Then a mis-tap changes nothing you need.
  • Footwear. Not a joke. Most mid gig phantom preset changes are actually the two arrow switches hit together by a wide boot.

Stomp mode, six switch versus four switch

Under Global Settings > Footswitches > Stomp Mode you choose whether all six switches are stomps, or four are stomps and the left pair shows Bank, Preset or Snapshot arrows. Four switch is the right call for a live rig because you get navigation without leaving Stomp mode. Six switch is right when a preset is genuinely a pedalboard and you change presets by hand.

Editing

Changing parameters

Pedal Edit mode and its hard ceiling

Hold MODE one second. You land on a block select screen. Pick a block and the six switches become an editor. Here is the whole layout, and the thing nobody tells you is that FS4 does both directions:

That is the ceiling. Pedal Edit is for adjusting one thing without bending over. It is not a performance tool and it is not a build tool, and the manual says as much.

What to use instead for hands free control

A switch that toggles a parameter

Press and hold the parameter's knob to jump into Controller Assign, pick the footswitch, set Min and Max, choose Momentary or Latching. Swap Min and Max to reverse it.

The classic use: one switch that raises Drive and lowers Channel Volume at the same time. That is a channel switch, built from nothing.

Snapshots carrying the values

Push and turn a knob to bind it to snapshots, then set a different value in each. One tap moves a dozen parameters at once with no zipper noise and no lost trails.

This is what working players actually use. Budget is 64 assignments per preset.

Timing

Tempo and the tuner

Tap floor
Tapped entry will not go below 40 BPM, deliberately, so fast taps are not misread. You can still dial down to 20 BPM by hand with the BPM parameter or in Global Settings.
One tap
A single press resets LFO phase. On a tremolo or a rotary that is how you land the wobble with the downbeat.
Tempo Select
Global Settings > MIDI/Tempo. Per Preset is the default and correct when one song is one tempo. Per Snapshot when a single song changes feel between sections. Global when you run to a house click across the whole set.
Note values
Press a Time or Speed knob to swap between ms or Hz and note divisions. Note divisions follow tap tempo and incoming MIDI clock. Some Speed and Rate parameters cannot be note synced because they are non-linear.
Re-tapping artifacts
Set Tap Tempo Pitch to Transparent in Preferences so delays stop making that seasick pitch glide when you change tempo mid song.
Tuner types
Hold TAP one second. Fine is the default needle with a precision bar, Coarse drops the bar, Strobe is the third option. Accurate down to 21.83 Hz for five string bass. All tuner settings are global.
Tuner output
Knob 1 sets what you hear while tuning: Mute for silence, Main L/R to keep playing, or Send L/R to feed an outboard tuner. Reference pitch is adjustable 425 to 455 Hz. String offsets live on pages 2 through 4 and only apply if you enable Offsets on page 1.
Worth knowing for bass

The onboard tuner reads more sensitively than most clip on tuners. Fine for stage. For setting intonation on a bass, reach for a dedicated strobe rather than fighting this one.

Expansion

Expression pedal and external switches

One jack, four possible things plugged into it. This is where the two stage setup catches everyone.

What gets assigned automatically

Add a Wah or Pitch Wham block and it binds itself to EXP 1. Add a Volume Pedal or Pan block and it binds to EXP 2. If you use the Mission SP1-L6H, its toe switch flips between the two.

Placement, and why it changes the feel

Volume before the amp

Rolling back cleans up the breakup, exactly like your guitar's volume knob. This is the musical choice and what most players set. Many cap the toe down max around 92% so there is still travel to push into saturation.

Volume after the amp

A clean master fader that does not touch tone at all. Correct for swells and for a stage volume control that will not change your sound.

Wah goes early, before drive and amp, because it needs to interact with the raw signal. After distortion it turns harsh and loses expression.

Beyond wah and volume

  • Auto engage a wah by pedal position. In Bypass Assign, set the block to engage past heel down and bypass on return. Different Position values per block means one pedal turns some things on and others off across its travel.
  • Crossfade parallel paths by putting the Split A/B Route To parameter on the pedal.
  • Put delay or reverb Mix or feedback on it for swells instead of another wah you never use.
  • Stack several parameters on one pedal for a graduated rhythm to lead move: volume up, mids up, delay feedback up, all in one sweep.
Best use of two external switches

Put Snapshot up and down on FS7 and FS8. That frees all six onboard switches for blocks, which is the single biggest capacity gain available to the XL. Each switch can hold up to eight assignments if you need more.

Half two

The rebuild

Your instinct is right. 128 factory presets built to demo 300 models will never be a rig, and their volume scatter is not a bug you can EQ around. But there is a correct order of operations, and one step you cannot skip.

Do this before you touch anything

Open HX Edit, File > Create Backup, save the .hxb somewhere you will find it in two years. Then separately drag any factory presets you might ever want onto your desktop as .hlx files.

Why both: restoring your backup later brings back the old factory presets, not the current ones. And HX Stomp XL does not support Bundle files, so preset by preset export is your only granular path. HX Edit can also pull individual items out of a backup without overwriting the unit, which is your escape hatch if you regret a wipe.

01

Get current, then reset once

Update to 3.80 or later through HX Edit. Then do one factory reset so you are starting from the improved factory presets and the newer cab engine rather than whatever shipped in the box. Firmware 3.50 rebuilt cabs and impulse responses to use roughly 66 to 80 percent less DSP than the old hybrid versions, which materially changes what fits in eight blocks.

On the XL the reset combination is footswitches C and D held while powering on. Not 2 and 3, which is the smaller HX Stomp. People miss this constantly and conclude their unit will not reset.

Verify before you hold anything down

Line 6 publishes several reset combinations and they do different things. Some keep your IRs, some erase them. Check the current Knowledge Base entry for your exact firmware before you commit, because there is no undo on the IR library.

02

Set the globals before the first preset

These are per unit, not per preset, so getting them right first means every preset you build inherits sane behavior.

  • Output level. Line for FRFR, monitors, or a DI to front of house. Instrument for the front of a real amp.
  • Send and Return level. Instrument for stompboxes in the loop. Line only if your amp's loop actually runs at line level.
  • Guitar In Pad. On if you have hot pickups. Without it you hit the amp models harder than they were voiced to be hit, and everything breaks up early.
  • Input impedance. Leave on Auto so it mirrors whatever block is first, the way a real pedal loads your pickups.
  • Snapshot Edits to Discard, Snapshot Reselect to Toggle, Tap Tempo Pitch to Transparent.
03

Build one template and never build from scratch again

This is the highest leverage step in the whole project. Make a single preset that fixes everything structural, then duplicate it for every new sound.

The template locks: input and gate settings, output level, standard block order, which switch means what, what each of the four snapshots means, and your standard controller assignments. On a dark stage you need FS2 to mean the same thing in every preset on the unit, and the only way that happens is if you never start from a blank.

Two mechanisms make this repeatable. Favorites saves a fully dialed block with its settings and assignments so you can drop your boost or your ambient reverb into any preset in one move, and they export as .fav files. User Model Defaults changes what a model looks like every time you insert it, so your go to delay starts at your settings instead of Line 6's. Build both libraries as you go.

04

Design in HX Edit, judge on the hardware

Exactly the split you proposed. HX Edit is where library work happens: reordering by drag, copy and paste of blocks between presets, renaming, batch export. The hardware is where you decide whether it needs more tremolo, because a 2.4 inch screen is a bad place to organize 128 slots and a computer is a bad place to judge a tone at volume.

One structural note: presets cannot be deleted into emptiness. There are 128 fixed slots and you overwrite them. A wipe is really writing your own content or blanks over the top, so build downward from 01A and stop when you run out of ideas worth keeping.

The fix for your loudest complaint

Level matching and gain staging

The control you are looking for

Channel Volume, not Master. Ben Adrian, the Line 6 engineer who models the amps, put it plainly: Channel Volume is always a flat, post amp level control, like a fader on a console. It sits inside the amp block but is not tied to the amp's tonality. Master behaves like a real amp's master volume, so turning it up adds power amp saturation and changes the sound.

So: Channel Volume is your leveling fader. Master is a tone control. That distinction alone fixes most preset volume scatter.

The method

  1. Pick your most used preset, usually the rhythm crunch, and call it the reference. Set its Channel Volume around 10 if it is your cleanest anchor.
  2. Play at real performance volume through the rig you actually use. Not headphones at 2am.
  3. Match every other preset to the reference with Channel Volume.
  4. Match the clean part of each preset, not the distorted part. Distortion compresses and lies to your ear.
  5. Use a meter, a DAW, or pink noise as a sanity check, never as the verdict.
Why the meter disagrees with your ears

The output indicators show peak level, not perceived loudness. A high gain preset with the same peak as a clean one will sound louder because it is compressed, and a mid forward preset sounds louder than a scooped one at identical dB. Expect to set high gain presets slightly lower by the meter so they sit right in the room.

Where clipping actually comes from

  • Leave per block Level at 0.0 dB. Boosting Level on several blocks in a row is the most common way people build digital clipping into a preset.
  • The Input, Output, Send and Return blocks carry signal present and clip indicators. Green is signal, red is too hot. If the Output block goes red, pull down Channel Volume or block Levels, do not just turn down the volume knob.
  • Use the Output block Level and Pan as a final trim, after Channel Volume, not instead of it.

Global EQ, and what it is not for

Three parametric bands plus low and high cut, applied to everything at the output stage. It exists to compensate for a room or a playback system, and it is the right tool when the venue is boomy. It is the wrong tool for fixing a preset, because a preset problem follows you to the next room.

The detail that matters for you specifically: Global EQ is never heard on the Send, Digital or USB outputs. It cannot contaminate your recordings or your direct feed. Reset it from the Menu, Global EQ, ACTION, Reset EQ, OK.

The cheese problem

Dialing effects so they do not embarrass you

Almost every cheesy modeler tone is one of two mistakes: Mix too high, or no filtering on the wet signal. Both are one knob away from fixed.

Mix is the honesty control

Mix is wet against dry. Level is how loud the block is. Reaching for Level when you mean Mix is how people end up with a reverb that sounds like a parking garage bolted onto a guitar. These are ranges working players land on in a full band mix, not laws:

EffectMix in a band mixNotes
Reverb15 to 30%Common pro approach is decay all the way up with low Mix and generous filtering, rather than short decay at high Mix. It will sound too quiet alone. Trust it in context.
Delay20 to 25%For supportive rhythmic delay. Higher only when the repeat is the part.
Chorus, flange, phaseModestExtreme mixes are the classic tell. Around 50% depth is a deliberate genre choice, not a default.
Ring mod and similar30 to 50%At 100% it is a novelty. Scaled back it becomes a usable broken radio texture.

Cut the wet signal so it stops fighting you

Most delay and reverb blocks have Low Cut and High Cut. This is the single most underused pair of parameters on the platform. Starting points that working players use:

  • Delays: Low Cut around 200 Hz, High Cut around 4 kHz. Darkening the repeats puts them behind your pick attack instead of competing with it.
  • Large reverbs: Low Cut around 500 Hz, High Cut around 5 kHz. Kills the mud under the band and the hiss on top.

The rest of the taming kit

Headroom
On some mod and delay blocks. Negative values add grit the way the original pedal did, positive values clean it up. At 0 dB the model behaves like the hardware.
Trails
On means repeats and tails ring out when you bypass the block or change snapshot. This is what makes snapshot changes sound seamless. Turn it on deliberately.
Spread
On stereo delays, 0 is centered and 10 is full left to right. On modulated stereo delays it sets LFO phase relationship instead, 0 in sync and 10 fully opposed.
Mono over stereo
A stereo block costs roughly twice the DSP of the mono version, and a dual cab roughly twice a single. If your rig is mono there is no audible reason to pay that. Free the DSP.
Legacy models
The Legacy subcategory holds the M13, M9, DL4 era models. They generally cost less DSP and are sometimes the better musical choice. Newer is not automatically better here.

Order changes the answer

  • Time based after the amp is clean and studio like. This is the default and usually correct.
  • Delay before the amp pushes the repeats through the gain stage so they compress and blur. More organic, washy under high gain. This is the Vox and Edge territory.
  • EQ before the amp shapes what hits it. Pete Thorn's documented trick is cutting everything below 500 Hz going into a high gain amp to clear the mud. EQ after shapes the final voicing.
  • Gates go at the input for the whole chain, or immediately after a high gain drive to catch that drive's own noise.
  • One amp into a dual cab or an IR beats two full Amp plus Cab blocks, for width at a fraction of the DSP.

Spending eight blocks well

What eats your DSP

Amps, impulse responses and reverbs are expensive. The polyphonic pitch models are in another category entirely: Poly Sustain, Poly Pitch, Poly Wham, Poly Capo, 12 String and Poly Detune can each consume up to half a preset's available DSP. If you want one, plan the preset around it from the start rather than discovering the ceiling at block six. Ben Vesco maintains per model DSP tables if you want the exact numbers.

Recording

The studio side

You already record. This part of the box is worth more to you than the footswitches, and the routing is the only confusing bit.

USB 1/2
Processed signal, and the default monitoring path. What you hear by default is hardware monitored from here.
USB 3/4
Additional monitoring returns. Point your Output block here when you want to hear DAW plugins on the armed track instead of the hardware's own monitor path.
USB 5/6
The only USB pair selectable as an Input block source. This is your reamp return path. 1/2 and 3/4 are monitoring only and cannot feed the chain.
Dry DI
Recorded simultaneously with the processed tone on a separate stream, so one pass gives you both a committed sound and something to reamp later.
Windows driver
Install the Line 6 Helix ASIO driver and select it in your DAW. Set the buffer as low as runs clean. Note that Windows allows only one active ASIO device, which complicates running the XL alongside your other interface.
Helix Native
Owners get a discount, and presets move between hardware and plugin. For reamping and detailed editing this is usually less painful than routing audio back through the box.

Reamping, the short version

  1. Record the processed tone and the dry DI on separate tracks in one pass.
  2. Route the dry track's output to USB 5/6.
  3. Set the XL's Input block source to USB 5/6.
  4. Record the result back on a new track.

Four cable method and hybrid rigs

4CM works, with one requirement people forget: you must place a Send/Return > Mono > FX Loop block at the point in the chain where your real amp's preamp belongs. Without it you get silence, not a bypassed loop. Factory presets starting with 4CM are wired for this and will not make sound otherwise.

For a hybrid rig where you want a real amp on stage and a modeled feed to the desk, put a Send block immediately before the Amp or Amp plus Cab block. The tap feeds your amp, the full modeled chain with cab or IR goes to front of house. Factory presets starting with BAS are built on this pattern.

Aux input, worth knowing

The Return jacks can be globally set as an always on stereo Aux In, and the Input block can be sourced from Return L/R. Between those two you have a path for the Nord, a drum machine, or anything else you want running through the effects without repatching. That is what 02D in the bank map above is for.

Honest accounting

Where credentialed people disagree

Presented as disputes rather than settled facts, because they are not settled.

Snapshots or stomps

Joe Gore argues for snapshots on tap efficiency. Alex Strabala uses stomps for the 32 combination range and changes presets by hand. Neither is wrong, and the split tracks how choreographed their gigs are.

Wipe or overwrite

Eric Klein's explanations of the reset mechanics suggest factory resets exist mainly to refresh factory content after updates. Plenty of players never wipe and simply build downward from 01A. Wiping is optional.

Amp plus Cab, or Amp plus IR

IRs often cost less DSP and third party ones are frequently more polished. Stock cab blocks give you real time mic and distance control. Genuine split, and after the 3.50 cab engine rewrite the DSP argument is weaker than it was.

How much reverb

Defended positions run from roughly 15% to 30% Mix in a mix. That spread is genre and taste, not a fact anyone can settle for you.

What is documented versus what is opinion

Documented behavior, from the owner's manual, HelixHelp and the Line 6 release notes: every button sequence in this document, what snapshots store, DSP costs, reset combinations, Global EQ routing, tuner specifications, the firmware timeline.

Opinion, attributed: the Mix percentages, the cut frequencies, the anchor amp shortlist, wipe versus overwrite, snapshot versus stomp philosophy. Ben Adrian's Channel Volume guidance sits in between, phrased as advice but coming from the engineer who built the models.

Firmware notes that matter

  • The XL shipped March 2021. Its manual is labeled 3.0 but it launched into the 3.10 era.
  • Snapshot Reselect, the row arrangement options and snapshot return behavior all arrived in 2.80 in 2019, so they were present from day one on your unit.
  • 3.50, November 2022, rebuilt the cab engine. New cabs and 1024 point IRs use roughly 66% less DSP than the old hybrid versions, and 2048 point IRs roughly 80% less. If your mental model of what fits in eight blocks comes from older advice, update it.
  • 3.80, November 2024, is current: six amps, four cabs, one effect added.
Before you trust this

Check these against your own unit

  • Reset combinations. C and D on the XL for factory restore, but the wider matrix of globals only, presets only and full wipe is documented for the HX Stomp family generally. Confirm against the current Knowledge Base for your firmware. A wrong combination can take your IR library with it.
  • The four center switch letters. The column logic in the control map is solid. Which of A, B, C, D sits on the front row versus the back is silkscreened on your pedal, so read it there rather than trusting a diagram.
  • Numeric effect settings. Starting points from experienced players, not specifications. Expect band context values to sound thin when you play alone.
  • The MODE switch still cannot be locked as of 3.80. If that changes in a later firmware the release notes will say so.
  • Power off before plugging anything into the EXP and FS jack. Live insertion can flip the tip and ring setting.
