For the Tarantino / De Palma Canon

A map of what Quentin Tarantino and Brian De Palma have actually cited, homaged, re-released, or ballot-listed: kung fu, giallo, blaxploitation, grindhouse, noir and heist, Melville-style French crime, Hong Kong heroic bloodshed, Hitchcockian thrillers, samurai, New Hollywood, and car-chase cinema. The Leone shelf sits this one out; in its place, the Cinema Speculation deep cuts and the rest of the Rolling Thunder catalog.

The documentary spine

Tarantino's Sight & Sound ballots (2002, 2012), his Grindhouse Cinema Database top 20, Cinema Speculation, the Video Archives and Bret Easton Ellis podcasts, and his Rolling Thunder Pictures re-releases. De Palma left no ballot, so his canon is reconstructed from interviews, his films' explicit homages, and the 2016 Baumbach and Paltrow documentary.

Where they converge

Hitchcock, film noir, and the idea of remixing genre. Tarantino on Blow Out: "one of the greatest movies ever made... Brian De Palma is the greatest director of his generation." Carrie was the only horror title on his 2012 ballot. Where a film has a hard documented connection, it is tagged; a handful ride on cult standing as connective tissue.

Rio Bravo

Howard Hawks · 1959 · USA

A Texas sheriff and a ragtag crew, a drunk, a cripple, a kid, hold a killer in jail against a rancher's army. Tarantino's self-declared "ultimate hangout movie": "if I ever liked a girl... I would show her Rio Bravo, and she better like it!" A stated template for Jackie Brown and Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, and #2 on his 2002 ballot.

A Confederate veteran spends years hunting the Comanche who abducted his niece, his racism curdling into obsession. Documented favorite of both directors; Tarantino devotes much of his Taxi Driver chapter in Cinema Speculation to its influence, and it appears among De Palma's cited favorites.

Where to start

The documented spine, if you want the load-bearing titles: Rio Bravo, Taxi Driver, Five Fingers of Death, Lady Snowblood, Deep Red, Coffy, Rolling Thunder, The Killing, Le Samouraï, City on Fire, Vertigo, and Vanishing Point. Every one is tied to a specific quote, ballot, homage, or re-release.

Deep-cut priorities for the obsessive who has seen the canon: Soul Brothers of Kung Fu, Detroit 9000, Pretty Maids All in a Row, The Mighty Peking Man, Thriller: A Cruel Picture, The Outfit, Hardcore, and Duel at Diablo.

Rolling Thunder completists: five of the label's six reissues are on this bill (The Beyond, Detroit 9000, The Mighty Peking Man, Sonatine, Switchblade Sisters). The sixth is Chungking Express, which sits outside the genre lanes here but belongs on your shelf anyway.

To skew harder toward De Palma: promote Body Double, Sisters, and Dressed to Kill to primary texts and add Eisenstein's Battleship Potemkin (the Odessa Steps he restaged in The Untouchables) and Powell and Pressburger's The Red Shoes.

Caveats

Attribution asymmetry: Tarantino's picks are documented to the individual title via his own lists and book; De Palma never submitted a Sight & Sound ballot, so his canon here is reconstructed from interviews, the 2016 documentary, and the explicit homages inside his films. Treat his broader favorites as "reportedly cited."

Connective tissue: a few titles (Duel at Diablo, The Big Bird Cage, Hard Boiled) lean more on cult standing and genre-representative value than on a single hard quote.

Porous boundaries by design: Battle Royale could sit under revenge or exploitation; The Street Fighter is Japanese but grouped with martial arts; High and Low is Japanese but grouped with crime because that is the lane in which De Palma cites it. The groupings reflect how these directors talk about the films, not rigid taxonomy.
